- What public holidays are there in Hungary?
- How do people celebrate national holidays?

### Customs, traditions

- What Hungarian Christmas traditions do you know?
- What Easter customs do you know?
- Are traditions disappearing? Why do you think so?
- Which traditions would you like to pass on to your children?

### Weekdays and holidays

- How are holidays different from weekdays?
- What do you usually do on public holidays?
- Do you prefer long weekends or one long summer break?

## 5. Minta felelet (Sample monologue)

> Today I would like to talk about holidays and public holidays in Hungary.
>
> First of all, family holidays. My favourite is Christmas. On Christmas Eve the whole family gathers at my grandmother's: we have fish soup and bejgli for dinner, sing carols, and then we unpack the presents under the decorated tree. Birthdays are also important in our family — we always have a birthday cake and my mother cooks the birthday boy's or girl's favourite meal.
>
> As for other holidays, Hungary has several national holidays. On 15 March there is a ceremony at our school, we wear a cockade and recite poems. On 20 August we watch the fireworks over the Danube. My cousins also celebrate foreign holidays, like Halloween, which my grandmother doesn't really understand.
>
> Speaking about customs, my favourite is the Easter tradition: boys sprinkle the girls with perfume or water, and the girls give them painted eggs. I think some traditions are disappearing, because young people spend more time online, so it is our job to pass them on.
>
> Finally, holidays are completely different from weekdays: no alarm clock, no school, we sleep in, visit relatives or go on trips.
>
> All things considered, I believe holidays are the glue that keeps families together. Thank you for listening.

## 6. Minta válaszok – ha másvalakiről beszélsz

### a) Családi ünnep – a nagymamád karácsonya

> For my grandmother, Christmas is a two-week project. She starts baking in mid-December: bejgli with walnut and poppy seed, gingerbread, and her famous plum jam. On Christmas Eve the whole family gathers at her house, and she is happy only if the table is bending under the food. She always says that presents don't matter — what matters is that everyone is at the table. And every year, when we sing carols together, she has tears in her eyes.

### b) Nemzeti ünnep – a nagypapád, az öreg katona? or friend's family at 15 March; use: a sógornő? Let's: az apukád március 15-én — hmm better someone else: a szomszéd család

> Our neighbours are real patriots. On every national holiday they raise the Hungarian flag on their balcony at eight in the morning, and their children wear cockades to school. On 15 March they take the whole family to the ceremony in the town square, and the grandfather tells the children the story of the 1848 revolution for the hundredth time. My mother jokes that the neighbours' flag collection is bigger than their wardrobe — but honestly, I find their dedication heart-warming.

### c) Szokás, hagyomány – a barátodéknál eltűnik a hagyomány (ellenpélda)

> My friend's family is not very traditional. They don't cook the classic Christmas fish soup — they order pizza —, and on Easter Monday nobody goes sprinkling. Instead, they celebrate "modern" holidays: they carve pumpkins at Halloween and give each other cards on Valentine's Day. My friend says traditions are not disappearing, they are just changing — maybe he is right, but I would miss the old customs, like carol singing or the Advent calendar.

### d) Hétköznap és ünnep – az apukád

> My father is a completely different man on public holidays. On weekdays he is a busy engineer: alarm at six, coffee, traffic jam, late dinners. But on a long weekend he doesn't set the alarm, he sleeps in until nine, and then he disappears in the garage with his fishing rod or goes to the football pitch with me. My mother says the best way to recognise a holiday in our family is very simple: it's the day when Dad is humming in the kitchen.

### e) Egy teljes tétel – a rokonság húsvétja a faluban

> I would like to tell you about Easter at my relatives' in the village, because for me it is the most beautiful holiday of the year.
>
> First, it is a real family holiday. More than twenty relatives gather at my great-aunt's house, and the children only meet some of their cousins on this day.
>
> As for other holidays, the weekend starts with a church service on Sunday, and on Monday the real fun begins.
>
> The customs are old and alive at the same time: in the village the boys still sprinkle the girls with cold well-water, not with perfume, and in return they get hand-painted eggs and a slice of ham. My great-aunt says the water brings health and beauty — the girls hope it works.
>
> Compared with weekdays, these two days are like a different world: no work, no school, only food, games and long conversations in the garden.
>
> All things considered, this is the holiday that makes me feel that traditions are still alive in Hungary. Thank you for listening.

## 7. Tippek a vizsgára (Exam tips)

- A dátumokat az *on* elöljáróval használd: *on 15 March, on Christmas Eve, on Easter Monday*.
- A magyar szokásokat magyarázd úgy, mintha a vizsgáztató sosem hallott volna róluk – ez a „magyarázat" készség pontot ad.
- A „disappearing traditions" kérdésnél mutass kétféle nézőpontot (on the one hand… on the other hand…), és zárjuk véleménnyel.
- Említs érzelmeket is: *I always look forward to…, it makes me feel…* – ettől lesz természetes a felelet.
