# Services – B szintű szóbeli tétel

## 1. Tétel és vezérfonal

**Topic: Services**

- catering (restaurants, cafés, catering in school and at work)
- repair and maintenance
- emergency services (ambulance, police)

---

## 2. Vezető kérdések (vizsgáztatói kérdések)

### Catering

- How often do you eat out?
- What is your favourite restaurant or café?
- What is the food like in your school canteen?
- Do you prefer fast food or proper restaurants?

### Repair and maintenance

- What do you do when something breaks down at home?
- Who repairs things in your family?
- Have you ever had a serious breakdown at home?
- Is it worth repairing old machines, or better to buy new ones?

### Emergency services

- What emergency services do you know?
- When should you call 112?
- Have you ever needed an ambulance or the police?
- How can we thank emergency workers for what they do?

## 4. Összekötő kifejezések (Linking phrases)

- **First of all, … / To begin with, …** – Mindenekelőtt…
- **Another thing is that …** – Egy másik dolog az, hogy…
- **On the one hand … on the other hand …** – Egyrészt… másrészt…
- **What is more, … / Besides, …** – Ráadásul…
- **All things considered, …** – Mindent egybevetve…
- **In my opinion / Personally, I think …** – Véleményem szerint…
- **It depends on …** – Attól függ, hogy…

## 5. Minta felelet (Sample monologue)

> Today I would like to talk about services: catering, repairs, and the emergency services.
>
> First of all, catering. We eat out once or twice a month, and my favourite place is a small pizzeria near our school, where the service is fast and the waiter knows our order by heart. About our school canteen — I'm honest: some days the food is great, other days we joke that the soup is "mystery soup". Still, it's cheap and hot, which is better than chips from the corner shop.
>
> As for repair and maintenance, in our family my father is the first "emergency service": he fixes almost everything himself, from the tap to the bike. When the washing machine broke down last year, it was still under warranty, so the mechanic came for free. I think maintaining things is cheaper than replacing them.
>
> Finally, emergency services. Two years ago my grandfather had a strong chest pain, so we called the ambulance — it arrived in ten minutes, and the paramedics were calm and professional. They probably saved his life. My sister's stolen bike case also ended well thanks to the police.
>
> All things considered, we only notice services when we need them — maybe we should say thank you more often. Thank you for listening.

## 6. Minta válaszok – ha másvalakiről beszélsz

### a) Vendéglátás – a nagynénéd, a kávézós

> My aunt has run a small café in our town for twelve years, and she knows more about people than a psychologist. She knows her regulars by heart: the pensioner who drinks the same coffee at seven sharp, the mother who comes in "just for a cake" every Friday after her diet week. She says the hardest part of the job isn't the coffee — it's standing eight hours and staying kind on a bad day. When customers leave a tip, she saves the coins in a jar labelled "for my staff's patience".

### b) Javítás – a nagypapád, a barkácmester

> My grandfather can repair almost anything, and his garage is a museum of screwdrivers and old radios. When my mother's vacuum cleaner "died", the shop said repairing it would cost more than a new one — my grandfather fixed it with a two-thousand-forint spare part and an afternoon of cursing. He also maintains the whole family's bikes every spring. His philosophy is famous in our family: "Throwing away is easy. That's exactly why repairing is worth more."

### c) Vészhelyzeti szolgálatok – a szomszéd, a mentőtisztnő

> Our neighbour Réka is a paramedic, and her working day is a different planet from ours. She works night shifts, sleeps at noon, and her phone alarm is an actual siren sound "to feel at home". She has saved lives on the motorway, in the snow, and once in a lift that got stuck between floors. When I once asked her how she deals with the stress, she said: "When the alarm rings, fear comes along. But the patient on the other end is more afraid than I am — so I forget mine and hold their hand first."

### d) Egy teljes tétel – a szomszéd, a mentőtisztnő

> I would like to describe our neighbour Réka's everyday life, because services appear in it at every point.
>
> First, catering: she works night shifts, so her "lunch" is a takeaway sandwich at three in the morning, and the hospital canteen is the only restaurant she visits regularly.
>
> As for repair and maintenance: her car is her second workplace, so she services it every three months — she says a broken ambulance can cost a life.
>
> And of course, emergency services are her profession. She always teaches us the two most important sentences: "Know the address when you call 112", and "Don't move an injured person unless you must".
>
> All things considered, Réka showed me that services are not "background noise": they are real people who keep our life running. Thank you for listening.

## 7. Tippek a vizsgára (Exam tips)

- Szerepjáték-mondatakat tanulj étteremhez és segélyhíváshoz — ebből a tételből szinte garantált a párbeszédes feladat.
- Szenvedő szerkezet a javításoknál: *The washing machine was repaired in two days* – itt természetes a passzív.
- A borravalóról és a menzáról legyen vicces, de kiegyensúlyozott véleményed – a humor felengedi a vizsga hangulatát.
- A 112 mellett említs konkrét tanácsokat is (say the address first, don't hang up) – ez mutatja a valódi tájékozottságot.
