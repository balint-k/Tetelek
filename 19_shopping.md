# Shopping – B szintű szóbeli tétel

## 1. Tétel és vezérfonal

**Topic: Shopping**

- daily shopping
- shopping habits (online, offline)
- shopping facilities (shopping centre, convenience store, market, supermarket)
- payment methods
- the role of advertisements

---

## 2. Vezető kérdések (vizsgáztatói kérdések)

### Daily shopping

- Who does the shopping in your family?
- Where do you buy bread and milk?
- How often do you go shopping?
- What do you never forget to buy?

### Shopping habits (online, offline)

- Do you prefer online or offline shopping? Why?
- What was the last thing you ordered online?
- What are the risks of online shopping?
- Do you like window shopping?

### Shopping facilities

- What shops are there in your town?
- Where do you buy vegetables — at the market or in the supermarket?
- What can you do in a shopping centre?
- What are corner shops good for?

### Payment methods

- How do you usually pay when you shop?
- What are the advantages of paying by card?
- Is contactless payment safe?
- Will cash disappear one day?

### The role of advertisements

- Do advertisements influence you?
- What do you think about influencers and ads?
- Are special offers real bargains?
- Should advertisements target children?

## 4. Összekötő kifejezések (Linking phrases)

- **Another thing is that …** – Egy másik dolog az, hogy…
- **On the one hand … on the other hand …** – Egyrészt… másrészt…

## 5. Minta felelet (Sample monologue)

> Today I would like to talk about shopping: my daily shopping, online and offline habits, shops, payment, and advertisements.
>
> First of all, in our family my mother and I do the shopping. Every Saturday we go to the market for fresh vegetables, fruit and eggs, because the products come straight from the farmers. During the week I'm the "emergency shopper": when we run out of bread or milk, I cycle to the corner shop.
>
> As for habits, I buy games and books online, because it's convenient and often cheaper. For clothes, I prefer real shops, because you can try things on — online I would have to return half of everything. Before ordering, I always read reviews.
>
> Speaking about facilities, our town has a new shopping centre with a supermarket, a cinema and dozens of smaller shops. It's exciting, but honestly, the market is still my favourite place.
>
> As for payment, I usually pay by card, contactless, because it's fast and I don't have to carry cash. At the market, however, cash is still king, and I always keep the receipt.
>
> Finally, advertisements. They try to tempt us all the time, and influencers make it even worse. My rule is simple: if I didn't need it before I saw the ad, I don't need it after.
>
> All things considered, shopping is easy today — the difficult part is not buying things we don't need. Thank you for listening.

## 6. Minta válaszok – ha másvalakiről beszélsz

### a) Napi vásárlás – a nagymamád piaci szertartása

> My grandmother has been going to the same market for thirty years, and for her it's not shopping — it's a social event. She knows every farmer by name, they know her by name, and the conversation is always longer than the purchase. She touches every tomato, smells every melon, and if the price is too high, she walks away twice before the deal is closed. When the new supermarket opened, everybody said she would switch. She didn't — as she says, "The tomatoes there don't have a name. Mine do."

### b) Online vs. offline – a nővéred, a webshop-specialista

> My sister does almost everything online: clothes, cosmetics, even her coffee cups come from webshops. She is a professional: she reads reviews for half an hour, checks five pages for prices, and orders with the calm confidence of a general. The only problem is the returns — every second parcel goes back, and our hallway looks like a small post office for weeks. When the delivery man rings twice a day, my father just sighs: "Could we order a bigger hallway too?"

### c) Üzletek – a nagyapád és a bevásárlóközpont

> When our new shopping centre opened, my grandfather went with us once, and it was like taking a nineteenth-century farmer to the future. He touched the escalator rail, counted the shops — thirty-seven — and couldn't understand why people buy "water from a bottle when there's a tap at home". His conclusion was surprisingly wise: "This is not a shop. It's a city under a roof — and in a city, you can spend money without even noticing." Now he stays with his beloved corner shop, which, by the way, has known his name for forty years.

### d) Fizetés – a nagymamád és a készpénz vs. a bátyád mobilja

> In our family payment methods show the generations perfectly. My grandmother pays only in cash — she says a card "hides the money, and you should see your money when it leaves". My parents use their bank cards with PIN codes. And my brother? He taps his phone on the machine like it's magic, and the payment is done before my grandmother has opened her purse. She is still suspicious about the phone thing — she once asked him: "And what happens if you have no battery? Do you wash the dishes then?"

### e) Egy teljes tétel – a nagymamád vásárlási világa

> I would like to describe my grandmother's way of shopping, because it covers every point of this topic — just in an old-school version.
>
> First, daily shopping: she shops every second day, never in bulk, because — she says — fresh food only stays fresh if you buy it fresh.
>
> Her habits are completely offline: she wants to touch the tomatoes, and the only "review" she trusts is the taste from last week.
>
> As for facilities, her kingdom is the market and the corner shop; the shopping centre is, in her words, "a place for people with too much money and too little time".
>
> She pays in cash, always, and she counts the change carefully, even today.
>
> And advertisements? She is immune. When I asked her why, she said: "An ad is a stranger shouting at your door. Why would I let a stranger decide what's for dinner?"
>
> All things considered, my grandmother taught me that smart shopping didn't start with the internet. Thank you for listening.

## 7. Tippek a vizsgára (Exam tips)

- A mennyiségi kifejezések aranyat érnek: *a loaf of bread, a carton of milk, a kilo of apples, a bunch of grapes* – ebből a tételből garantáltan kérdeznek ilyet.
- Szerepjátékra is számíts: *Can I help you? – I'm just looking, thanks. / How much is it? / Can I pay by card?*
- Az online vásárlásnál a biztonság kulcsszavai: *reliable shop, reviews, secure payment, keep the receipt*.
- Az áraknál múlt időt gyakorolj: *It cost 2,500 forints. / I paid by card.*
