### Online administration

- What official matters can you arrange online in Hungary?

## 5. Minta felelet (Sample monologue)

> Today I would like to talk about digital communication: devices, apps, social media, and online administration.
>
> First of all, devices. I use my smartphone for almost everything — alarm clock, camera, messages and music —, and my laptop is for schoolwork. Honestly, my screen time is too high, about five hours a day, so I try to take "phone-free" walks with the dog.
>
> As for applications, my favourites are a messenger, a music app and, believe it or not, the banking app, because it saves queuing. For school we use a timetable app and I always check my emails there.
>
> Speaking about social media, it has two faces. On the one hand, it keeps me in touch with my friends and my favourite bands. On the other hand, there are dangers: fake news, scams, cyberbullying, and the fact that people share too much private information. My rules are simple: strong passwords, two-step verification, and I never accept strangers.
>
> Finally, online administration. My parents arrange almost everything on the government portal: taxes, car papers, appointments with the offices. It saves a lot of time — you only have to protect your data well.
>
> All things considered, digital communication is a great servant but a bad master: we should use it, not obey it. Thank you for listening.

## 6. Minta válaszok – ha másvalakiről beszélsz

### a) Eszközök – a nagypapád és az első okostelefon

> Last Christmas we gave my grandfather his first smartphone, and it has been an adventure series ever since. The font size is set to "giant", and he still answers the phone with a formal "Hello, Kéri speaking" even when it's just a WhatsApp message notification. His proudest moment was making his first video call — he held the phone at arm's length and inspected my aunt's kitchen like a customs officer. He now says the smartphone is "the magnifying glass of the century", and he checks the weather on it hourly, just to have something to disagree with.

### b) Alkalmazások – az unokatesóved, aki alkalmazást írt a nagymamának

> My cousin Peti studies programming, and last year he wrote a small app for our grandmother — a simple medication reminder with big green buttons and a loud bell. Our grandmother was suspicious for two weeks ("What if the phone forgets?"), but now she shows it to everyone like a medal. The app has three functions: it rings, it shows a picture of the pill, and it sends a message to Peti when she takes it. It won't win any prize, but in our family it counts as proof that technology can be warm.

### c) Közösségi média – a barátod és a híres márkás cipőátverés

> My friend Bendegúz learned the dangers of social media the expensive way. He saw an advertisement for "branded" trainers at half price on a social media site, liked the page, trusted the reviews, and paid instantly. The shoes arrived a month later — from a brand nobody has ever heard of, one size too small, with the logo already peeling off. When he wrote a complaint, the page had disappeared like a ghost. Now he is our family expert on safe shopping: real shop, real website, never pay by bank transfer to a stranger.

### d) Online ügyintézés – az édesanyád, az online adminisztrátor

> My mother handles the family's official matters like a professional: taxes, doctor's appointments, car papers, school certificates — everything happens on the government portal, often before breakfast. She says the biggest advantage is the queue: there isn't one. My grandfather, who spent half his youth standing in corridors waiting for stamps, watches her in disbelief: "So the office came to the kitchen?" — Exactly, Grandpa. Of course, my mother is careful too: she never saves her passwords on a shared computer, and she checks every page address twice.

### e) Egy teljes tétel – a nagymamád digitális éve

> I would like to tell the story of my grandmother's digital year, because it touches every point of this topic.
>
> First, devices. The family bought her a tablet, and for a month it was "the expensive picture frame" — then the grandchildren taught her video calls, and it became a window to the family.
>
> As for apps, she uses three: video calls, weather, and a recipe app that she trusts more than her own notebook.
>
> Speaking about social media, she joined the family group — and almost fell for a scam message about a "free tea set for grandmothers". Since then, she asks before clicking, and she is proud of it.
>
> And online administration: my mother arranges her official matters together with her, on the tablet, at the kitchen table. My grandmother calls it "the office with a cake" — and honestly, that is exactly what it is.
>
> All things considered, my grandmother's story proves that it is never too late to enter the digital world — just take a grandchild with you. Thank you for listening.

## 7. Tippek a vizsgára (Exam tips)

- Élményeknél present perfect: *I have never been hacked, I have used it for two years* – a „tapasztalat" nyelvtana.
- A digitális biztonságnál tanácsokat is adj: *You should use strong passwords, you mustn't share your data* – ez a modális segédige vizsgapontja.
- Használj ellenpontot: *On the one hand it connects people, on the other hand it isolates them* – a social media téma erre épül.
- Egy konkrét történetet készíts elő (átverés, első videóhívás) – a történet mindig többet ér, mint az általánosság.
