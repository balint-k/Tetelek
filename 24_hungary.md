## 2. Vezető kérdések (vizsgáztatói kérdések)

### Domestic destinations

- Which Hungarian destinations would you recommend to a foreigner?
- What is your favourite place in Hungary?
- Where do Hungarians usually spend their summer holidays?
- Which regions of Hungary have you visited?

### Places of interest

- What can you see in Budapest?
- Which Hungarian sites are on the UNESCO World Heritage list?
- What do you know about Lake Balaton or Hollókő?
- What would you show a tourist in your own town?

### Programme opportunities in different seasons

- What programmes can you do here in spring, summer, autumn and winter?
- What festivals or events do you know?
- Which Christmas fair is famous in Budapest?
- What do you do at weekends in the different seasons?

### Hungarian specialities

- What is Hungarian cuisine famous for?
- Which dishes would you make a foreigner try?
- What drinks is Hungary famous for?
- What makes Hungary unique — beyond the food?

## 5. Minta felelet (Sample monologue)

> Today I would like to talk about Hungary: destinations, sights, seasonal programmes and our specialities.
>
> First of all, destinations. If a foreigner has one week, I would send them to Budapest first, then to Lake Balaton in summer, and to Hollókő, a beautiful old village on the World Heritage list. My personal favourite is the Balaton area, because the "Hungarian sea" has a special atmosphere in every season.
>
> As for places of interest, the capital is full of them: the Parliament by the Danube, Buda Castle, the Chain Bridge and the breath-taking view from the Fisherman's Bastion. Outside Budapest, the Hortobágy, the Pannonhalma Abbey and the cave baths of Miskolc-Tapolca are also worth visiting.
>
> Speaking about the seasons, Hungary is programme-rich all year: spring is for hiking, summer for the Balaton and open-air festivals, autumn for wine festivals and the harvest, and winter for the Budapest Christmas fair, ice-skating and — best of all — the thermal baths in the cold.
>
> Finally, our specialities. Hungarian cuisine is famous for goulash, lángos and chimney cake, and our wines, especially Tokaj, are world-famous. But the best Hungarian speciality is hospitality: we always feed the guest until he can't move.
>
> All things considered, Hungary is a small country that has everything — you just have to taste it. Thank you for listening.

## 6. Minta válaszok – ha másvalakiről beszélsz

### a) Úti célok – az amerikai unokatesó egy hete

> Last summer my American cousin visited us for one week, and we planned his "Hungary tour" like a military operation: Budapest for two days, then Eger, then the Balaton. He arrived expecting "a small grey country" — and left with a full camera and a new favourite city. What surprised him most? Not the Parliament, but the fact that he could swim in a lake warmed like a bath, and that the lángos lady at the beach remembered his order on the second day. His conclusion on the airport bus: "You Hungarians don't advertise your country enough. It's better than the ads."

### b) Látnivalók – a nagybátyd, az amatőr idegenvezető

> My uncle has never worked as a tour guide, but every guest who visits our family gets "the uncle tour" — and no one escapes it. The route is always the same: the Parliament from the outside ("we'll not queue for the inside, the outside is the real thing"), a walk across the Chain Bridge ("hold the railing, feel the history"), and a hot chocolate with the view of the Fisherman's Bastion. He has told the same stories for twenty years, and strangely, it never gets boring — because he doesn't tell them like a guide, but like a proud grandfather.

### c) Szezonális programok – a nagymamád falujának szüretje

> Every autumn, my grandmother's village celebrates the grape harvest, and for me it is the best programme of the whole year. The whole street works together in the vineyards, the children sneak the sweet grapes, and in the evening there is a long table in the yard with homemade wine, bread and goulash. There is folk music, the mayor gives a speech nobody listens to, and the oldest neighbour dances with the youngest child. My city friends who visited once still talk about it — and this simple village festival convinced them more about "real Hungary" than any brochure.

### d) Specialitások – a nagymamád főz az angol csere-diákra

> When our English exchange student stayed with us, my grandmother treated the visit like a national mission. On the first day the poor girl got goulash soup, chicken paprikash and — "for the English taste" — a tray of kürtőskalács. She photographed everything like a food journalist and asked for the recipes, which was a problem, because my grandmother cooks with "some of this" and "enough of that". The only thing she couldn't win her over with was the hot lángos with garlic: "Delicious", she said politely, and drank a litre of water. The goodbye note on the fridge said: "Thank you. I came for the language, but I will remember the food."

### e) Egy teljes tétel – az amerikai unokatesó szemszögéből, teljes

> I would like to tell you about my cousin Matt's week in Hungary, because a foreigner's eyes show our country differently.
>
> First, the destinations. We planned a classic route: Budapest, Eger and the Balaton — and Matt loved each one for a different reason: the views, the castle and the "warm lake".
>
> As for places of interest, his favourite wasn't the famous Parliament but the small street of Hollókő, where — as he said — "the houses look like they tell stories". That sentence made me see my own country differently.
>
> Speaking about programmes, he visited us in summer, so he got the full summer package: swimming, open-air cinema, and a village football match where he played as the "American guest of honour".
>
> And the specialities: he tried everything, learned three Hungarian words, and all of them were food names — gulyás, lángos, kürtőskalács.
>
> All things considered, Matt's week taught me the most important thing about Hungary: it is easy to love, you just have to see it once through a guest's eyes. Thank you for listening.

## 7. Tippek a vizsgára (Exam tips)

- Ajánlásnál mindig mondd a miértet: *You should visit the Parliament because it's the most beautiful building by the Danube.* – a *because* nyelvi és tartalmi pont.
- Gyakorold a magyar helységnevek angol kiejtését — a vizsgáztató előtt mondania kell a Balatonnak, a Hollókőnek.
- Készülj a négy évszak programjaival — a tételsor kifejezetten kéri a szezonális felosztást.
- Egy személyes élmény (pl. egy Balaton-nyár, egy kirándulás) felér tíz statisztikát.
