# Target language country/countries – B szintű szóbeli tétel

## 1. Tétel és vezérfonal

**Topic: Target language country/countries (United Kingdom, USA)**

- knowledge about the country/countries
- experiences
- lessons learned

---

## 2. Vezető kérdések (vizsgáztatói kérdések)

### Knowledge about the country/countries

- What do you know about the United Kingdom?
- What are the most famous sights in London?
- What do you know about British traditions and customs?
- What differences are there between the UK and the USA?

### Experiences

- Have you ever been to an English-speaking country?
- Have you ever met a native speaker in Hungary?
- Have you ever been to a language camp?
- How do you use English in your everyday life (films, games, internet)?

### Lessons learned

- What can we learn from English-speaking countries?
- What did you (or your family) learn from meeting foreigners or travelling?
- What should Hungarians adopt from these countries — and what not?
- Has learning English changed you?

## 4. Összekötő kifejezések (Linking phrases)

- **First of all, … / To begin with, …** – Mindenekelőtt…
- **Another thing is that …** – Egy másik dolog az, hogy…
- **On the one hand … on the other hand …** – Egyrészt… másrészt…
- **What is more, … / Besides, …** – Ráadásul…
- **All things considered, …** – Mindent egybevetve…
- **In my opinion / Personally, I think …** – Véleményem szerint…
- **It depends on …** – Attól függ, hogy…

## 5. Minta felelet (Sample monologue)

> Today I would like to talk about the countries of the English language: what I know about them, my experiences, and the lessons I have learned.
>
> First of all, knowledge. The United Kingdom is a monarchy with four parts: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Its capital, London, is famous for Big Ben, Buckingham Palace, Tower Bridge and the red double-deckers. British people are famous for their politeness, their afternoon tea and their endless conversations about the weather. The USA is different in many ways: bigger distances, more cars, dollars instead of pounds, and a completely different school system.
>
> As for my experiences, I have never been to London yet — but English is around me every day: films with subtitles, games, music, and the internet. Two years ago I went to an English language camp, where the teachers were native speakers, and on the last evening we had a "British tea evening" — the tea was good, our accents were terrible, and everybody laughed until midnight.
>
> Speaking about lessons learned, these experiences taught me three things: speaking is more important than perfect grammar, politeness costs nothing, and being wrong is not a tragedy — staying silent is.
>
> All things considered, the English-speaking world is not so far away: it enters my room every day through my headphones. Thank you for listening.

## 6. Minta válaszok – ha másvalakiről beszélsz

### a) Ismeretek – a nővéred, az anglomán

> My sister is a real Anglophile: she watches British detective series without subtitles, drinks tea at five, and knows the map of London better than the map of our own town. She can explain the difference between the UK, Great Britain and England in one breath, and she once corrected our history teacher about the number of British kings called Henry. Her dream is a semester at a London university, and she is already saving money — in a jar decorated with a hand-drawn Union Jack. My father jokes that she is not learning English; she is slowly moving to England by bus.

### b) Élmények – az unokatesóved londoni nyelvtábora

> Last summer my cousin spent three weeks at a language school in London, and she came home like a different person. The first week was hard: her host family spoke fast, the Underground map terrified her, and she called home crying because she "didn't understand anything". Then something changed: she started asking, laughing at her own mistakes, and travelling alone in the city. By the third week she was negotiating in a second-hand bookshop and arguing about football with a Scottish classmate. Her summary of the trip: "I went to learn the language. I came back with a second personality."

### c) Tanult leckék – az édesapád üzleti útja az USA-ban

> My father spent two weeks in the USA on a business trip years ago, and he still tells the stories like stand-up comedy. He learned about tipping the hard way (the waiter's face after his 50-cent tip still hunts him), about small talk ("How are you?" is a greeting, not a question), and about distances — his partner's "just next door" office was a two-hour drive. But his biggest lesson was positive: he said Americans don't laugh at your mistakes, they help you finish your sentence. He came home braver in English than after ten years of school.

### d) Egy teljes tétel – a nővéred londoni tervei (vagy az unokatesóved három hete) — teljes

> I would like to tell you about my cousin Eszter's three weeks in London, because her story covers everything: knowledge, experience and lessons.
>
> Before the trip, her knowledge of the UK came from series and books: she knew the sights, the monarchy and the traditions — on paper.
>
> The reality gave her experiences the screen couldn't: a host family with two cats and three house rules, real afternoon tea, and the discovery that the English really do talk about the weather — twice per conversation.
>
> The lessons learned were the real souvenirs. She learned that politeness opens doors, that a mistake with a smile is worth more than a perfect sentence with fear, and that being alone in a big city teaches you more about yourself than about the city.
>
> She came home with a suitcase full of tea bags and a new sentence for life: "Comfort zones are nice to visit, but you shouldn't live there."
>
> All things considered, Eszter's trip showed me what a language is really for: not for tests, but for doors. Thank you for listening.

## 7. Tippek a vizsgára (Exam tips)

- Tudd a különbséget: *England ≠ the UK* — a vizsgáztató szívesen ellenőrzi, így England, Great Britain, the United Kingdom pontos jelentését.
- Élményeidnél present perfect: *I have never been to London, I have met native speakers* – ez a „tapasztalat" nyelvtani ruhája.
- Terveidhez feltételes: *If I had the chance, I would spend a semester in Scotland.* – szinte kikönyöröghetetlenül jön ez a kérdés, legyen rá mondatod.
- A „lessons learned" pontra készíts 2-3 konkrét, rövid tanulságot — a generális bölcsességeknél a vizsgáztató mindig a konkrétumot szereti.
